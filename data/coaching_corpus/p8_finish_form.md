# P8 — Finish — Form

## What this position is

The Finish position (P8) is the final static checkpoint of the golf swing — the position the golfer holds after all rotational energy has been spent and the club has come to rest behind and above the lead shoulder. It is not a position the golfer consciously manufactures; a balanced, high finish is the natural result of correct sequencing and full body rotation through the impact zone and followthrough.

The finish is diagnostically valuable precisely because of this: a golfer who struggles to reach a high, balanced P8 typically has a rotation or weight transfer problem earlier in the swing. Coaches often use the ability to hold the finish position for two seconds as a proxy for overall swing balance and sequencing quality.

## Body posture

At P8, the body has fully rotated toward the target. The hips are typically 80-100° open from their address position — essentially facing the target or slightly past it. The shoulders have continued to rotate through and are also facing the target, approximately 80-100° from their address orientation. The torso is fully upright or even tilted slightly toward the target — the spine angle from vertical is typically 5-20° (considerably more upright than at address), as the body has risen through the release.

Weight is almost entirely on the lead foot — 95-100% — with the trail foot balanced on its toe. The trail knee has driven all the way to the lead knee and the two knees are roughly touching or close together. The lead leg is straight and acts as the axis of rotation. The overall body silhouette is a tall, rotated posture facing the target — sometimes described as a "trophy pose."

## Arm and club position

The club has swung up and over the lead shoulder and come to rest behind the golfer's neck and lead shoulder. The lead elbow is folded upward, with the lead arm approximately parallel to the ground or slightly above it, and the club shaft pointing roughly behind and above the head — typically 60-80° from horizontal. The lead forearm has rotated and the back of the lead hand faces roughly skyward in a high finish.

The trail arm is also folded, with the trail elbow pointing downward and the club shaft resting comfortably on the trail shoulder area. The grip end of the club points roughly toward the target or slightly left of it. Players with more upright swings typically finish with the club very high behind the head; players with flatter swing planes may finish with the club more around the lead shoulder. Both are acceptable style variations provided the balance and rotation quality are correct.

## Typical pro ranges

- Spine angle from vertical: 5-20° (significantly more upright than address)
- Hip rotation: 80-100° from address (facing target)
- Shoulder rotation: 80-100° from address (facing target)
- Lead arm angle at finish (above horizontal): 0-30°
- Club shaft angle from horizontal: 60-80° (high finish)
- Weight distribution: 95-100% lead / 0-5% trail
- Trail foot: balanced on toe tip, heel fully raised
- Trail knee: adjacent to or touching the lead knee
- Balance: position holdable for 2+ seconds

## Why it matters

The finish position is the final quality check of the entire swing. A golfer who finishes high, balanced, and facing the target with their weight fully committed to the lead side has almost certainly sequenced the swing correctly. Conversely, a short finish (club stopping near shoulder height), a falling-back finish (weight on the trail side), or a loss of balance indicates a problem in the preceding positions. Because the finish is easy to observe and compare across swings, coaches often use it as the primary qualitative measure of swing improvement during a lesson.
