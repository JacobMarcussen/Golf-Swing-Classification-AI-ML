# P1 — Address: Form

## What this position is

The Address position is the static stance the golfer takes before initiating the swing. It is the foundation of the entire motion — every subsequent position is influenced by how the body is set up here. The golfer is stationary, with the club behind the ball and the body in a balanced, athletic posture ready to rotate.

## Body posture

The feet are roughly shoulder-width apart for a driver, with the lead foot turned out slightly toward the target. Weight is distributed evenly between both feet, with a slight emphasis on the balls of the feet rather than the heels — the golfer should feel athletic and ready to move, not flat-footed.

Knees are slightly flexed, around 15-25° of knee bend. Hips are tilted forward from the waist, creating a forward bend at the hip joints (hip flexion) of approximately 30-45° from vertical. The spine maintains a relatively straight line from hips to head, tilted forward to match the hip flexion.

Shoulders are square to the target line — neither closed nor open. The lead shoulder sits slightly higher than the trail shoulder due to the natural offset of the hands on the grip, creating a small spine tilt away from the target of around 5-10°.

## Arm and club position

Arms hang relatively naturally below the shoulders, with the lead arm (left for a right-handed golfer) extended toward the ball and the trail arm slightly bent at the elbow. The hands are positioned just ahead of the ball with a driver, with the shaft leaning very slightly toward the target (or vertical, depending on style).

The club shaft is angled downward to the ball, with the clubhead resting behind the ball. The shaft is approximately vertical when viewed from the face-on perspective — typical shaft angles range from -5° to +10° from vertical (where 0° is straight down).

## Typical pro ranges

- Knee flex: 15-25° from straight
- Spine angle from vertical (forward bend): 30-45°
- Hip line and shoulder line: roughly horizontal, both square to target
- Lead arm: extended, near-straight (170-180° elbow angle)
- Trail arm: slightly bent (140-170° elbow angle)
- Shaft angle from vertical: -5° to +10°
- Weight distribution: 50/50 to slightly favoring the lead side

## Why it matters

A faulty Address position propagates errors through the entire swing. Excessive forward bend leads to early extension as the golfer tries to make room for the swing. Insufficient knee flex restricts hip rotation. A tilted shoulder line at address pre-loads the swing in a way that's hard to correct mid-motion. Most coaches consider Address responsible for 60-70% of the issues seen later in the swing.
