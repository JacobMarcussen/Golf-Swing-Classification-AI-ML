# P7 — Mid-followthrough — Common Faults and Fixes

## Chicken wing (lead elbow breaking outward)

**What it looks like:** After impact, the lead elbow bends and flares outward away from the body — resembling a chicken wing — rather than staying in a straight-line extension through the shaft. The lead arm collapses and the club is pulled around the body on a steep, outside path. This is one of the most common followthrough faults in amateur golf.

**Detection signal:** `left_elbow` angle substantially below 150° at P7; `left_shoulder` elevated or pulled backward; `shaft proxy angle` showing an abrupt elevation rather than a smooth arc; the shaft and lead arm are not forming a line when viewed from behind.

**Why it happens:** Usually caused by a scoop or open-face impact — the body instinctively pulls the arm inward to prevent the clubface from rotating further open. Also caused by a lack of forearm rotation through impact. In some cases it is a self-taught compensatory move to handle a path that comes from outside.

**Fix:** Focus on maintaining lead arm extension through the impact zone. Cue: "push the club head toward the target after the ball" rather than pulling it around. Drill: make slow half-swings focusing on keeping the lead arm in line with the shaft through P7. A mirror positioned face-on confirms whether the chicken wing is present.

## Incomplete forearm rotation (open face followthrough)

**What it looks like:** At P7, the club toe is not pointing upward — instead the face is still partially open, with the toe pointing toward the target or the leading edge facing upward. The forearms have failed to rotate through the impact zone, leaving the face open.

**Detection signal:** Club toe angle showing the face significantly open at P7 (toe angle substantially less than 80° from horizontal); `left_shoulder` and `right_shoulder` indicating the arms have not crossed over; ball flight data typically showing fade or slice curvature.

**Why it happens:** Insufficient forearm rotation during the release. A weak grip contributes to this fault, as does a conscious holding-off of the clubface to prevent hooking. Also common in players who were told to "keep the face open" through the ball.

**Fix:** Allow the trail forearm to rotate over the lead forearm naturally through impact. Cue: feel the trail forearm cross over the lead forearm by P7. Drill: make swings with a water bottle placed under the lead armpit; as the club comes through, allow the arms to rotate and release the bottle toward the target.

## Excessive forearm rotation (closed face followthrough)

**What it looks like:** At P7, the club toe has rotated well past vertical — pointing away from the golfer (past 12 o'clock) — indicating the forearms have over-rotated through impact. This is associated with excessive draw or hook spin.

**Detection signal:** Club toe angle showing the face excessively closed at P7 (toe angle greater than 110° from horizontal); `right_elbow` and `left_elbow` showing an aggressive crossover; ball flight typically showing right-to-left hook spin.

**Why it happens:** A very strong grip causes the face to close rapidly through impact. Also caused by a compensatory "flip" when the body stalls — the hands take over and rotate violently to square the face.

**Fix:** Check grip neutrality — a neutral grip reduces the tendency to over-rotate. Ensure the lower body continues rotating through impact so the hands don't have to do extra work. Drill: make slow-motion followthroughs and check that the toe points straight up at P7, not past it.

## Collapsed followthrough (arms folding too early)

**What it looks like:** The arms fold inward toward the body immediately after impact rather than extending out toward the target. The followthrough is short, compact, and abruptly stops near the P7 position. This typically produces shots that lack distance and tend to be pulled left.

**Detection signal:** Both `left_elbow` and `right_elbow` showing significant flex at P7; `shoulder_line_from_horizontal` not rotating sufficiently; `hip_line_from_horizontal` stalling; overall body rotation slowing prematurely as seen in the shoulder and hip values.

**Why it happens:** The golfer decelerates through impact — a very common issue with chips and pitches that bleeds into full swings. Also caused by swinging with the arms only (no body turn to carry the club through), leaving the arms to collapse when momentum runs out.

**Fix:** Commit to finishing the swing — the ball is a checkpoint, not the destination. Cue: "chase the ball with the club head." Drill: make full practice swings without a ball, focusing on the extension of the arms toward the target through P7 before wrapping into the finish.

## Body stall (hips stop rotating at impact)

**What it looks like:** The body rotation stalls at or just after impact — the hips stop clearing and the shoulders spin out to compensate. At P7, the hips are less open than expected and the shoulders are significantly more open, reversing the kinematic chain.

**Detection signal:** `hip_line_from_horizontal` showing minimal additional rotation from P6 to P7; `shoulder_line_from_horizontal` rotating aggressively past the hips; `left_hip` and `right_hip` showing the body "blocked" rather than continuing to clear through.

**Why it happens:** The golfer fires the upper body through the ball and the lower body acts as a passive pivot rather than continuing to drive. Often caused by a misunderstanding of how to use the hips — rotating them up to impact but then stopping.

**Fix:** Feel the hips continuing to rotate through P7 and toward P8. Cue: "belt buckle faces the target by P7." Drill: make swings where the trail foot steps through toward the target line after impact (step-through drill); this forces continuous hip rotation all the way through the followthrough.
