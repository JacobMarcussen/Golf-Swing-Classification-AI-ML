# P1 — Address: Common Faults and Fixes

## Excessive forward bend (over the ball)

**What it looks like:** Spine angle from vertical greater than ~50°. The golfer is hunched over the ball, head low, shoulders rounded.

**Detection signal:** `spine_angle_from_vertical` substantially above the typical 30-45° range, often 50-65°.

**Why it happens:** Usually a setup habit — the golfer reaches for the ball rather than standing tall and letting the arms hang. Sometimes caused by setting up too close to the ball.

**Fix:** Stand taller at address. Set the club behind the ball first, then step into the stance — the distance to the ball should be set by where the club naturally lies, not by how far the golfer chooses to bend over. Drill: place a club on the ground perpendicular to the target line; address position should leave the hands about a hand's width above this club without crowding it.

## Insufficient knee flex (locked-out legs)

**What it looks like:** Knees nearly straight at address, often paired with a very upright torso. The golfer has no athletic readiness in the legs.

**Detection signal:** Knee angles approaching 175-180° (essentially straight).

**Why it happens:** Common in beginners who feel awkward in an athletic posture, or in golfers who associate the swing with arms only.

**Fix:** Add knee flex consciously — the golfer should feel like they could jump from the position. Drill: assume the address position with the eyes closed, then open eyes and check that knees are bent enough that a slight downward push wouldn't tip the golfer forward.

## Open or closed shoulder line

**What it looks like:** The shoulder line is not parallel to the target line at address. An "open" shoulder line points the lead shoulder toward the target (left of target for a righty); a "closed" line points it away.

**Detection signal:** `shoulder_line_from_horizontal` substantially different from 0° (or 180°), or notably different from the hip line.

**Why it happens:** Often comes from gripping the club without re-checking alignment, or from compensating for a perceived swing flaw (e.g., aiming left to counter a slice).

**Fix:** Check alignment with a club laid on the ground parallel to the target line. The shoulder line should be parallel to that reference. Drill: practice setting up to a chair or wall; the lead shoulder should not extend further forward than the trail shoulder relative to the wall.

## Reverse spine tilt (leaning toward target)

**What it looks like:** The spine tilts toward the target rather than away from it at address. The trail shoulder sits higher than the lead shoulder — the opposite of what good form requires for a driver.

**Detection signal:** Spine angle from vertical with the wrong sign, or trail shoulder visibly higher than lead shoulder in face-on view.

**Why it happens:** Often a misunderstanding of how to set up to a teed ball, or carryover from iron play (where shoulder tilt is closer to neutral) without adjusting for driver.

**Fix:** With a teed driver, the lead shoulder should be slightly higher than the trail shoulder, creating a 5-10° tilt away from the target. This pre-sets the upward strike on the ball needed for an effective drive. Drill: hold a club along the shoulders and check the angle in a mirror or video.

## Weight overly on heels

**What it looks like:** Golfer rocked back onto the heels at address, often paired with a too-vertical posture or fear of the ball.

**Detection signal:** Hard to detect from pose alone; usually requires foot pressure data. Can sometimes be inferred from an unusually upright torso combined with hips pulled back.

**Why it happens:** Standing too upright shifts weight rearward; some golfers consciously avoid weight on the toes due to balance concerns.

**Fix:** Athletic posture with weight on the balls of the feet, not the heels. Drill: have the golfer try to lift both heels slightly off the ground while in the address position; if they can't, weight is too far back.
