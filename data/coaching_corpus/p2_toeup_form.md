# P2 — Toe-up — Form

## What this position is

The Toe-up position occurs early in the backswing when the lead arm has rotated to roughly horizontal and the clubhead has risen to approximately hip height. The name refers to the clubface orientation: ideally, the leading edge of the club is roughly vertical (toe pointing upward) when the shaft is parallel to the ground. This position is the first major checkpoint of the backswing and is used to assess whether body rotation and arm swing are working in sync.

P2 sits between the initial takeaway (P1) and the mid-backswing (P3). It is a transitional checkpoint rather than a held position, but its geometry has a strong influence on swing plane and clubface angle through the rest of the backswing.

## Body posture

At P2, the hips have begun to rotate away from the target, typically 10-20° from their address position. The shoulders are more actively rotating, usually 30-45° from address. The torso has turned as a unit, with the spine angle from vertical maintained close to the address value of 30-45° — any early loss of spine angle is flagged here.

Weight begins shifting toward the trail side, typically 55-65% on the trail foot by this point. The lead knee tracks inward (toward the trail leg) as a natural consequence of hip rotation. The trail knee retains most of its original flex, around 15-25°. The lead arm is driven by shoulder rotation rather than an independent arm lift — players who lift the arms without turning the body tend to show a club that is too far inside or outside the ideal path.

## Arm and club position

The lead arm (left for a right-handed golfer) is approximately horizontal or slightly below, extending away from the body as the shoulders rotate. The lead elbow should be close to straight — typical range is 165-180°. The trail arm begins to fold at the elbow, though minimally at this stage, with the trail elbow angle typically 150-170°.

The club shaft should be approximately parallel to the ground and to the target line when viewed from behind. The wrist hinge is minimal at P2 — most of the wrist cock happens between P2 and P3. The clubface, when the shaft is horizontal, should have the leading edge close to vertical (toe up), indicating a neutral rotation of the forearms. A closed face (toe pointing toward the ground) signals excessive forearm rotation inward; an open face (leading edge angled toward the golfer) suggests insufficient rotation or a very strong grip.

## Typical pro ranges

- Spine angle from vertical: 30-45° (maintained from address)
- Hip rotation from address: 10-20°
- Shoulder rotation from address: 30-45°
- Lead arm angle (elbow): 165-180° (near-straight)
- Trail arm angle (elbow): 150-170°
- Shaft angle from horizontal: ±10° (roughly parallel to ground)
- Clubface toe angle: 80-100° from horizontal (near-vertical, toe up)
- Weight distribution: 55-65% trail / 35-45% lead

## Why it matters

P2 is the first real diagnostic checkpoint of the swing. Errors in the takeaway — an inside or outside path, excessive wrist rotation, a collapsed lead arm — are all clearly visible at toe-up. Because the swing is still moving relatively slowly here, faults at P2 tend to compound through P3 and P4 as the body accelerates. A correct P2 position puts the club on a good plane for the remainder of the backswing and sets up the wrist hinge and shoulder turn that define the quality of the P3 and P4 positions.
