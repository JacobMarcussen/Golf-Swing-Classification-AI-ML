# P7 — Mid-followthrough — Form

## What this position is

The mid-followthrough position (P7) occurs after impact when the lead arm has again reached roughly horizontal — the mirror of the P5 position, but now on the target side of the swing. The club has passed through the ball and is swinging up and around the body. P7 sits between impact (P6) and the finish (P8) and is sometimes called the "toe-up on the followthrough" position, because in a correct swing the club toe points upward here just as it did at P2 on the backswing.

Although the ball is already gone by P7, this position is diagnostically valuable. The shape of the followthrough is a direct consequence of how the clubhead was delivered at impact — faults in impact (open face, chicken wing, early extension) all leave visible signatures in the P7 position.

## Body posture

By P7, the hips have rotated substantially toward the target — typically 55-70° open from address — and the torso has followed. The shoulder line is approximately square or slightly open to the target line, 30-50° open from address. The spine angle has begun to reduce compared to address as the body rises through the follow-through; most players are in the range of 15-30° from vertical at P7, becoming more upright than at address.

Weight is predominantly on the lead foot — 90-100% — and the trail foot has risen onto the toe, with the heel fully elevated. The trail knee has driven significantly inward and toward the target line. The lead leg is straightening and becoming the axis around which the body rotates. The overall body shape viewed from behind shows the torso facing the target or just past it.

## Arm and club position

The lead arm is approximately horizontal, extending toward the target. Critically, the lead arm should be in a straight-line extension from the shaft or very close — the "lead arm and club forming a line" is a key check for whether the lead arm has maintained structure through impact or collapsed (chicken wing). The lead elbow angle is typically 160-180° at P7.

The trail arm has fully extended through impact and is now also near-straight, with the trail elbow angle around 150-175°. The club toe is pointing upward (the mirror of P2), confirming that the forearms have rotated through impact in a neutral fashion. If the face is still open at P7 (toe pointing toward the golfer), insufficient forearm rotation through impact is indicated. The shaft angle from horizontal is typically 10-30°, with the grip end pointing roughly at the golfer's trail hip.

## Typical pro ranges

- Spine angle from vertical: 15-30° (more upright than address due to followthrough rise)
- Hip rotation (open): 55-70° from address
- Shoulder rotation (open): 30-50° from address
- Lead arm angle (elbow): 160-180°
- Trail arm angle (elbow): 150-175°
- Club toe orientation: 80-100° from horizontal (near-vertical, toe up)
- Shaft angle from horizontal: 10-30°
- Weight distribution: 90-100% lead / 0-10% trail
- Trail heel elevation: fully raised off ground

## Why it matters

P7 functions as a post-impact audit. A correct P7 indicates that the clubface was square through the impact zone and that the forearms rotated naturally through the ball. A chicken wing at P7 (lead elbow bent outward) indicates the lead arm broke down at or after impact, which is almost always associated with scooping or an open face. An incorrect club toe angle (face still open) at P7 reveals that the forearms failed to rotate. Because P7 is more observable than the impact instant itself, coaches often use it as a proxy for what happened at P6.
