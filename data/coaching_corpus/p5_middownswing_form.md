# P5 — Mid-downswing — Form

## What this position is

The mid-downswing position (P5) occurs when the lead arm has returned to roughly horizontal on the way down and the club shaft is approximately parallel to the ground — the mirror position of P3 on the backswing, but with the club now descending. This is one of the most critical positions in the swing because it determines the club's approach angle and path into the ball at impact (P6). The slot — the inside-out delivery position — is either achieved or lost at P5.

P5 is a dynamic position; the club is moving at high speed and the body is actively generating force. The key observable features are the lag angle between the shaft and lead arm, the shaft approach plane, and the body position relative to the ball.

## Body posture

By P5, the lower body has led the downswing sequence. The hips have rotated back toward the target and have cleared significantly — typically 20-40° open (toward the target) relative to address, while the shoulders are only 20-40° open (having "held back" relative to the hips). This hip-shoulder separation in the downswing is the primary source of the kinematic chain that delivers speed to the clubhead.

Spine angle from vertical is maintained close to address (30-45°), but a very common fault — early extension — sees the hips thrusting toward the ball as the spine angle decreases. Weight has shifted aggressively back to the lead side, with 75-90% on the lead foot. The lead knee is straightening from its backswing flex position, and the trail knee is driving inward and toward the ball. The trail heel may begin to rise slightly as weight moves forward.

## Arm and club position

The lead arm is approximately horizontal and extending toward the ball. The lead elbow is near-straight, 160-180°. The trail elbow has folded inward and close to the body — trail elbow angle is typically 75-95° at this point, and the elbow points down and inward toward the trail hip rather than outward. This is the classic "in the slot" position: the trail elbow tucked and the club approaching from inside the target line.

Crucially, the wrist hinge (lag) is largely preserved at P5. A properly delayed release will show the shaft angle from the lead arm still at 60-90° — the lag angle has not been "cast" away. The shaft, when viewed from behind, should approach from inside the target line, with the club head below the hands. The shaft angle from horizontal is typically 20-45° (pointing down at a steep angle toward the ball, shallower for a driver than for an iron).

## Typical pro ranges

- Spine angle from vertical: 30-45° (maintained from address)
- Hip rotation (open, toward target): 20-40°
- Shoulder rotation (open, toward target): 0-20° (significantly less than hips)
- Hip-shoulder separation in downswing: 20-35°
- Lead arm angle (elbow): 160-180°
- Trail arm angle (elbow): 75-95°
- Lag angle (shaft-to-lead-arm): 60-90° (lag preserved)
- Shaft approach angle from horizontal: 20-45°
- Weight distribution: 75-90% lead / 10-25% trail

## Why it matters

P5 is where ball-striking quality is most directly determined. A club that arrives at P5 from the inside, with lag preserved and the body in a good rotation pattern, will almost certainly produce a quality strike. A club that arrives steep, from outside, or with the lag already spent (cast) will struggle to produce consistent compression regardless of what happens in the final few milliseconds before impact. Coaching interventions at P5 have immediate, measurable effects on ball flight.
