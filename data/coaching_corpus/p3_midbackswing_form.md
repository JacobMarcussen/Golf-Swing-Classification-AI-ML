# P3 — Mid-backswing — Form

## What this position is

The mid-backswing position (P3) occurs when the lead arm has risen to approximately parallel with the ground and the club shaft has rotated to point roughly upward — often described as the "9 o'clock" position of the backswing. At this point, the wrist hinge is actively developing, the shoulders are well into their rotation, and the club is on its way to the top. P3 sits between the toe-up checkpoint (P2) and the top of the backswing (P4).

This is one of the most commonly analyzed positions in professional coaching because it clearly reveals whether the swing is on the correct plane, whether the wrist hinge is correct, and whether the body is rotating or just swaying.

## Body posture

By P3, the hips have rotated 25-45° from their address position. The shoulders have turned significantly more — typically 60-75° from address — creating the shoulder-hip separation (X-factor) that generates swing power. This differential stretch in the torso is one of the primary sources of stored elastic energy in the golf swing.

Spine angle from vertical is maintained close to the address position of 30-45°. A common deviation here is early extension, where the hips drift toward the ball and the spine angle decreases. Weight has shifted further onto the trail side, now typically 65-75% on the trail foot. The trail knee maintains its flex (15-25°), acting as a brace against which the hips can rotate. The lead knee has moved inward and may flex slightly more than at address.

## Arm and club position

The lead arm has risen to approximately horizontal or just above. The lead elbow remains close to straight, typically 160-180° — a significant bend here (below 150°) indicates a breakdown in the lead arm structure and usually results in a loss of power and plane. The trail elbow has folded to approximately 90-110°, with the upper arm staying relatively close to the torso.

Wrist hinge is now well established: the lead wrist has hinged so that the club shaft is approximately perpendicular to the lead arm when viewed from face-on, forming roughly a 90° angle at the lead wrist. The clubface should be roughly parallel to the lead arm (matching the spine angle is a common coaching reference). The shaft, viewed from behind, should point up toward the swing plane — ideally toward or just outside the trail shoulder line.

## Typical pro ranges

- Spine angle from vertical: 30-45° (maintained from address)
- Hip rotation from address: 25-45°
- Shoulder rotation from address: 60-75°
- X-factor (shoulder-hip differential): 25-40°
- Lead arm angle (elbow): 160-180°
- Trail arm angle (elbow): 90-110°
- Lead wrist hinge: 80-100° (approaching full cock)
- Weight distribution: 65-75% trail / 25-35% lead

## Why it matters

P3 is where swing plane problems become most visible and where the consequences of P2 faults are amplified. A club that was slightly off-plane at P2 is clearly off-plane at P3. The wrist hinge established here largely determines the shaft angle at the top (P4), and the shoulder-hip separation drives the potential power available in the downswing. Coaches frequently use P3 as a primary teaching reference because it is slow enough to analyze yet consequential enough to correct meaningful issues.
